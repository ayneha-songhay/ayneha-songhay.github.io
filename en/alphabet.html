The AYNEHA alphabet

An alphabet with no upper or lower case, read and written from right to left: 5 vowels, 22 consonants, 4 diacritics and 10 digits. With all the forms, the font has 98 characters.

Letters Diacritics Digits Punctuation Writing rules

The system at a glance

The 98 characters of the Ayneha Pro font fall into five families. Only the 27 basic letters make up the alphabet; the others are forms of these letters or signs.

The 4 diacritics

Diacritics are placed above or below a letter and change its sound. A character never carries more than one diacritic at a time.

􏾢􏾤􏾧􏾩􏾬

Nasalisation

Two dots lying above the vowel. It gives ã, ẽ, ĩ, õ, ũ: the vowel goes through the nose.

􏾥􏾪

Openness

A wavy line above E and O only. It gives ɛ (as in “bed”) and ɔ (as in “law”).

􏾡􏾭􏿏􏿁

Double

Two bars under the letter. The vowel is lengthened, the consonant is doubled. All letters take it except H. A double consonant is always followed by a vowel.

􏾮􏾼􏿀􏿌

Silent

A small upside-down L under the consonant. It marks a consonant that is not directly followed by a vowel: at the end of a word, or before another consonant. Ʃ, TƩ, J, Ɲ and H never take it.

The 10 digits

AYNEHA has its own digits. Like letters, a number is read from right to left: the first digit is on the right. For example, 2026 is written 􏽾􏽼􏽾􏾂: the 2 of the thousands is on the far right, the 6 of the units on the far left.

Three digits echo the shape of Latin digits, redrawn with right angles: 0 (a rectangle), 3 and 9.

For decimal numbers, the AYNEHA comma sits at the bottom. For an ordinal (first, second...), a small “i” is placed at the top, right after the number.

The 22 writing rules

These rules describe how AYNEHA characters behave together. AYNEHA is young: abbreviations and handwriting habits are left to usage.

  1. Right to left: AYNEHA is written and read from right to left, letters and digits alike.
  2. One form per letter: there is no upper or lower case.
  3. One sound, one character: each sound retained in the system (Songhay of Gao) has its own character. Ʃ, TƩ, DJ, Ɲ and Ŋ are single letters, not combinations.
  4. The joining line: all the letters of a word are joined by the same baseline.
  5. Alphabetical order: vowels first (A, E, I, O, U), then consonants, from the front to the back of the mouth.
  6. Order of forms: each letter is followed by its forms, always in this order: basic, nasalised, open, double, silent.
  7. Spelling names: vowels are said as they are, and “a” is added to consonants: ba, pa, ma, fa, wa...
  8. One diacritic only: a character never carries more than one diacritic at a time.
  9. Nasalisation: two dots above, on the 5 vowels only.
  10. Openness: a tilde above, on E and O only (Ɛ and Ɔ).
  11. Double: two bars below, on all letters except H. A double consonant is always followed by a vowel: it never ends a word.
  12. Silent: a small upside-down L below. A consonant is silent when it is not directly followed by a vowel: alone at the end of a word, or before another consonant.
  13. Letters that are never silent: Ʃ, TƩ, J, Ɲ and H never take the silent mark: these consonants are never found at the end of a word. H is the only letter with no diacritic at all.
  14. Open and nasalised vowels: since only one diacritic is allowed, nasalised Ɛ is written Ẽ and nasalised Ɔ is written Õ.
  15. Numbers: they are written from right to left: the digit with the highest value is on the right.
  16. Three familiar digits: 0, 3 and 9 take the shape of Latin digits, redrawn with right angles (0 is a rectangle).
  17. Ordinals: a small “i” is placed at the top, right after the number: 1st, 2nd, 3rd...
  18. Decimal comma: it is the AYNEHA comma, placed at the bottom.
  19. Punctuation: the comma and semicolon have their own AYNEHA character; the question mark is mirrored (⸮); other signs (full stop, exclamation mark, quotation marks, brackets) are the international ones.
  20. Mathematics: the whole expression is read from right to left; exponents and indices go at the top left; variables and functions (x, y, sin, log) stay in Latin letters; directional signs (<, >, arrows) are mirrored.
  21. No word breaks: since its letters are joined, a word is never split at the end of a line.
  22. No bold, no underline: they hide or distort the diacritics; to emphasise a word, write it larger. Next to Latin text, AYNEHA is written one point larger (13 for 12).
